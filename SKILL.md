# X Posts That Get Read

Write something a specific reader wants to finish and finds useful enough to like or pass along. Earn attention through substance, clarity, and voice. Do not promise reach or optimize toward a fictional points formula.

## Establish the brief

1. Use the user's current instructions and supplied material first. Identify the audience, topic, intended outcome, voice, quantity, format, and length.
2. Proceed with reasonable defaults when a brief is sufficient. Ask only for information that materially changes the output. For an unspecified batch, produce five distinct standalone posts, mostly concise with one longer worked example if it adds value.
3. Follow known topic and style preferences when available. For this user, default to practical AI, agents, SaaS, distribution, and founder work; make posts informative, lightly witty, and grounded rather than dominated by news. Use a named product only when requested or supported by context. Never invent its features.
4. Use first person only for experiences or opinions supported by the user's material. Do not fabricate customers, revenue, failures, experiments, conversations, credentials, or performance numbers. Frame an invented scenario as hypothetical.
5. Respect the requested length. If no posting entitlement or length is known, keep standalone posts within the standard 280-character limit where practical. Count before claiming exact compliance; do not confuse words with characters, and account for X's weighted handling of URLs and some Unicode when exact fit matters. Offer a clearly identified longer format or thread when necessary.

## Build each post

1. Choose one reader problem and one useful takeaway. Prefer a concrete observation, decision rule, tradeoff, example, small process, or grounded opinion.
2. Open with a specific tension, observation, or consequence. State enough of the idea that a reader gets value without tapping. Avoid withheld payoffs and exaggerated promises.
3. Develop the idea with a mechanism or example. Use details that help a reader do something, recognize a situation, or reconsider a decision.
4. Keep the middle moving: remove repetition, throat-clearing, and sentences that only restate the hook. Longer posts need more substance, not more suspense.
5. End with a useful payoff: a practical action, decision test, memorable observation, or genuine question that follows from the post. Do not append a question to every post.
6. Use natural paragraph lengths and mobile-readable spacing. Avoid breaking every sentence into theatrical fragments. Vary rhythm across a batch.
7. Make each post original and substantially different from the others in premise, opening, structure, and payoff. Treat word-for-word reuse as poor editorial practice; do not claim it always triggers a specific penalty.

Use [references/editorial-patterns.md](references/editorial-patterns.md) when drafting varied batches or repairing generic copy. Adapt patterns rather than copying examples.

## Edit for the user's voice

- Use plain language and concrete nouns. Allow a dry joke or familiar frustration when it serves the idea.
- Remove generic praise, filler, motivational platitudes, corporate jargon, canned revelation language, and repetitive hooks.
- Avoid default patterns such as “Everyone is doing X wrong,” “Let that sink in,” “Here's the truth,” “game changer,” and repeated “It's not X, it's Y” constructions.
- Avoid emoji decoration, hashtag stuffing, gratuitous mentions, manufactured outrage, and “like/repost/comment YES” requests. Use a relevant hashtag only when the context warrants it or the user asks.
- Do not force contrarianism or a sales pitch. Introduce a product only when it helps explain a real use case.
- Rewrite robotic copy for specificity and usefulness. Never claim to make AI text undetectable or guarantee avoidance of platform labels.

## Handle replies, profiles, and video

### Replies

Read the parent post before composing a reply. If it is unavailable, obtain its text or clearly offer a generic starting point. Add one relevant example, nuance, practical answer, or sincere question. Prefer one or two concise sentences. Avoid empty agreement, paraphrasing the parent, mass-produced variations, unsolicited pitches, unrelated links, and repeated replies beneath the same large account. Draft in the user's voice and invite a personal detail only when needed. Do not imply every AI-assisted reply is penalized or advise detector evasion.

### Profiles and pinned posts

Make the bio clear about the audience, topic, and value. Make the pinned post a useful introduction, example of expertise, product walkthrough, or resource consistent with recent posts. Use genuine evidence and relevant, trustworthy links. Do not assert an account-wide penalty from a pinned link without current evidence.

### Video

Show the problem, result, or useful visual immediately. Deliver the promised value and use captions when helpful. Choose length for the content and audience; do not pad to an alleged eligibility threshold. Treat opening, retention, and completion as distinct outcomes. A zero weight for one video-open signal does not make video worthless.

## Plan publishing and learn from results

- Use audience analytics and the audience's timezone before suggesting posting windows. Without analytics, label a proposed window as a test. Never claim 3 a.m. is universally bad.
- Favor a few distinct, strong posts over bursts of near-duplicates. Do not invent a universally optimal frequency or minimum gap. If the user requests a dense schedule, fulfill the brief and briefly explain the attention tradeoff when useful.
- Leave room to answer substantive replies after publishing. Never organize coordinated likes, reciprocal engagement schemes, or follow-backs solely to manipulate reach.
- Compare similar posts over multiple attempts. Track likes and meaningful replies relative to impressions, relevant follows, shares, profile visits, and qualified leads where available. Track dwell or completion only if the analytics expose them; do not fabricate them.
- Treat rates on tiny samples cautiously. Change one main variable in an experiment, such as the opening or posting window, and evaluate against the user's goal. Do not infer algorithm causation from one post.
- Produce drafts by default. Publish, schedule, send replies, change a profile, or create a recurring task only when the user's request authorizes that action and the destination is resolved. Use a connected posting tool when available; do not claim an action succeeded without confirmation.

## Keep algorithm reasoning honest

Read [references/algorithm-evidence.md](references/algorithm-evidence.md) before citing numbers, making an algorithm explanation, or giving a recommendation dependent on a threshold or label. For ordinary drafts, apply the durable editorial guidance without repeating the algorithm discussion.

- Verify volatile platform details against current primary sources when needed. Record the date, source file, commit if available, and the conditions under which a rule applies. Use a commit diff before asserting an exact change date or “highest ever.”
- Distinguish code-observed behavior, the user's supplied claims, and editorial inference. Do not equate public defaults with a guarantee across production experiments or all X surfaces.
- Treat ranking coefficients as multipliers on viewer-specific predictions, including some continuous values. Never calculate points per observed action, equivalents between likes and reports, a predicted reach score, or an expected rank from raw counts.
- Preserve uncertainty when sources are unavailable. Continue drafting useful posts without pretending the supplied thresholds are verified.

## Deliver and check

Return ready-to-use copy in the requested quantity and format. Separate posts cleanly; keep strategy notes outside the copy and omit them unless requested or needed to flag an important factual issue. Use a writing block when supported. Give alternatives only when requested or a genuine choice helps.

Before delivery, check every post for a clear audience, concrete value, honest support, a hook that matches the payoff, natural voice, appropriate length, and a distinct premise. Remove engagement bait and stale algorithm claims. If reviewing an existing draft, provide the rewrite first and a brief explanation of the meaningful edits. Do not invent a numeric quality or algorithm score.
